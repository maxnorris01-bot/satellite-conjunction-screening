## The sun module (`src/globe/sun.ts`)

- **Source:** satellite.js already ships `sunPos`, David Vallado's low-precision solar formula
  (documented at 0.01 degrees for 1950-2050), in the same inertial frame as the SGP4 positions. The
  module wraps it rather than adding a formula.
- **Reusable functions:**
  - `sunDirectionEci(date)` and `sunDirectionScene(date)`: the unit vector in the globe's frame;
  - `subsolarPoint(date)`;
  - `sunElevationDeg(lat, lon, date)`.
- **Checked against astronomy-engine,** an independent ephemeris added as a devDependency for the
  tests only. It isn't in the bundle: the globe chunk grew 3 kB in commit 1.
  - **Across 2,000 moments in 2026,** the worst subsolar error was 0.004 degrees in latitude and
    0.012 degrees in longitude.
  - **Reference values:**
    - subsolar latitude 0.0017 degrees at the March equinox (2026-03-20 14:45:36 UTC);
    - 23.4358 degrees at the June solstice (2026-06-21 08:25 UTC);
    - subsolar longitude within 4.5 degrees of 0 at 12:00 UTC in every month (the equation of
      time).

## Commit 1: globe day/night

- **Lighting:** a shader replaces the Earth's plain material and lights the surface in world space
  with the Sun's direction. The Sun is fixed in the inertial frame and the mesh already turns by
  GMST, so the shading follows the clock at 1x/10x/50x and the slider with nothing rotated by hand.
- **Look, by the Sun's elevation at each point:**
  - **Day side:** unchanged.
  - **Terminator:** a soft band from +3 to -12 degrees, about civil plus nautical twilight, roughly
    1,700 km on the ground. No hard line.
  - **Night side:** keeps 8% of the day texture, so land stays faintly visible, plus city lights
    fading in from 0 to -10 degrees.
  - The shading weights live in `src/globe/daynight.ts`; the GLSL is generated from the same
    constants the tests use.
- **City lights: NASA Black Marble 2016**, color map.
  - **Source:** "Global 3600x1800 (0.1 degrees)" from NASA's "Earth at Night/Black Marble: Flat
    Maps" page (https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/); file
    https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/imagerecords/144000/144898/BlackMarble_2016_01deg.jpg
    (779,638 bytes).
  - **Committed** as `public/textures/earth-night-2k.jpg`: 2048x1024, JPEG quality 70, 193,456
    bytes.
  - **Credit:** the source page has no credit line, and the original Earth Observatory article now
    redirects. NASA's media guidelines say NASA imagery "generally [is] not subject to copyright in
    the United States" and that "NASA should be acknowledged as the source of the material". It's
    credited next to the existing Blue Marble credit as "city lights: NASA Earth Observatory (Black
    Marble 2016, Suomi NPP VIIRS)", and in the README.
  - **Alignment:** with the day texture, measured. The share of bright city pixels that fall on
    day-texture ocean is lowest at zero horizontal shift (8.4%, rising either way). Vertically, the
    minimum is at -1 px (0.18 degrees, half-pixel resampling). London, New York, Tokyo, Cairo, São
    Paulo and Sydney all read near full brightness at their coordinates.
  - **Masking the base:** the map has a faint grey-blue land base under the lights (linear
    0.002-0.035, cities 0.26-0.97). Adding all of it made the night side look overcast, so texels
    fade in from 0.03 to 0.12 and only the lights are added.
- **Load order:** the lights start loading only after the day texture arrives, so the globe still
  appears as soon as before. Until they arrive, or if they fail, the night side is plain darkened
  day texture. Checked by blocking the request: no errors, darkened night.
- **Switch:** a "Day/night" checkbox in the legend (Globe only), default on. Off is the old
  uniformly lit globe. Compared pixel by pixel against the old material in the same frame, 1 pixel
  of 2.4 million differed.

### Legibility (the validator's contrast maths)

- **Method:** the rendered Earth colours were computed from both textures with the shader's own
  maths:

  | Side | Darkest | Median | 95th percentile | Brightest |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Lit | `#000000` | `#041638` (ocean) | `#b4b8b8` (cloud, ice, desert) | `#c4c4c4` |
  | Night | `#000000` | `#00020b` | `#363739` | `#fffffd` (city-light cores) |

- **Points:** every point colour, in all four modes (Type, Owner, Flat, Age share the same colours),
  separates from every background through its fill or its dark `#05070d` rim at 3:1 or better:
  - lit median, fill 4.6-10.6:1;
  - bright lit, rim 10.1:1 or more;
  - night median, fill 5.3-12.3:1;
  - night 95th percentile, fill 3.1-7.1:1;
  - city cores, rim 20:1.
- **Lines and rings (no rim):**
  - **Night median:** 6.0-20.7:1, better than the lit side. The orbit line is 9.8:1 vs 8.4:1.
  - **Brightest city-light cores:** 1.0-2.1:1 (the selection ring 3.5:1). The cores are tiny (the
    night 95th percentile is `#363739`), so a line crosses one for a few pixels.
  - **Bright cloud, ice and desert:** 1.0-2.0:1, as before day/night.
  - **Proposed fix (to-do, not applied):** a 1 px dark outline on rings and lines, like the points'
    rim.
- **Unaffected:** labels and the legend sit on their own opaque backgrounds.

## Commit 2: Sky twilight

- **The Sun in the observer's frame:** the same sun vector, projected onto the observer's
  east/north/up axes (`directionLookAngles` in `src/globe/sky.ts`). Its elevation agrees with the
  spherical formula to under 0.3 degrees (geodetic vs geocentric vertical).
- **Phases** (`twilightPhase` in `src/globe/twilight.ts`): daytime from -0.833 degrees (the
  conventional sunrise/sunset), then civil to -6, nautical to -12, astronomical to -18, and night.
- **Sky:** a dome just outside the grid, blended from horizon to zenith, with colours keyed to
  solar elevation. There's a warm glow low on the horizon toward the Sun's azimuth, from about -12
  to +10 degrees.
- **Sun marker:** a labelled soft disc while the Sun is up. It's never part of the pick buffers;
  clicking it picked nothing.
- **Panel:** "Your sky" gains the phase and elevation as plain text, e.g. "Civil twilight · Sun
  2.0° below the horizon". It isn't in any live region. During 3 s of 50x playback the one live
  region (the selection announcer) changed 0 times while this line kept updating.
- **Daytime cap: `#223956`.** That's the brightest blue at which:
  - every point colour, ring and line keeps 3:1 (orange 3.02, blue 3.23, green 3.45, grey 3.62,
    Flat 7.00, orbit line 5.53, selection ring 3.39, station ring 7.37);
  - the grid labels keep 4.5:1 or more (5.14 and 9.92).

  Against the lighter ground `#26303c` they're at least 3.44:1. The glow is scaled down so it
  never exceeds the cap; the tests check every elevation from -90 to +90. Over a full-day render
  sweep, the brightest sky pixel was exactly `#223956`.
- **Satellites aren't hidden in daylight;** physical visibility is a later feature.

## What looks off

- **The glow reads mauve rather than orange.** The cap that keeps dots legible leaves little room
  for warm colour. It's subtle by design.
- **London's noon Sun sits on the geostationary row** near the 30 degree label in October. That's a
  geometric coincidence; the dots draw on top of the disc.
- **Rings and lines over city-light cores** (and over bright cloud by day, as before): see
  Legibility.
- **On a phone in Globe mode**, the legend with the new Day/night row takes a little more of the
  stage. It wasn't changed; the existing layout.
