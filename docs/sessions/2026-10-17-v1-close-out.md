## Sky dimming when looking at the Earth, and its final values (#34, #37)

#34 added the dimming; the previous note recorded its first values (sunlit: glow x0.20, stars x0.45;
night: glow x0.50, stars x0.75) as "may be retuned". They were. After tuning by hand with the Sky
tuning panel, #37 shipped these as `SKY_DIM_DEFAULT` in `skydim.ts`:

| Setting | Final default |
|---|---|
| Glow, Earth in view, sunlit | 1.00 |
| Stars, Earth in view, sunlit | 1.00 |
| Glow, Earth in view, night | 0.19 |
| Stars, Earth in view, night | 0.19 |

This reverses the first design (strongest dimming in daylight): with the Earth in view in daylight
the dimming now does nothing, and it only acts over the night side. #37 checked the labels against
a known Sun and satellite position (the sunlit weight is 1 where the Sun is up at the sub-satellite
point and 0 where it is dark). The mechanism is unchanged: how much of the view is Earth is the
fraction of a cap of the view's field of view that lies inside the Earth's disc, through a
smoothstep from 5% to 60%, eased over 400 ms (1 s under reduced motion). Only brightness changes.

## Glow softening and the Sky tuning panel (#35, #36, #37)

- **#35** made the glow read as a faint band: baseline gain 0.5, saturation 0.7 to 0.45, gamma 1.5
  to 1.3, and a 1 degree Gaussian blur of the map built in the background.
- **#36** added the tuning panel. With `?debug=1` the Satellite view gets a "Sky tuning" tab (a
  bottom sheet on a phone) with sliders for the glow (gain, saturation, gamma, blur), the
  Earth-in-view dimming (glow and stars, sunlit and night), the stars (brightness, point size, and a
  limiting-magnitude offset for wide fields) and, from #39, the maximum field of view. It has
  Reset to defaults, Original look and Copy settings (JSON). The sliders write the same engine
  settings the console hooks (`__globe.*`) do. Values are remembered in `localStorage` in debug mode
  only; the panel's code is a lazy chunk never fetched without `?debug=1`.
- **#37 final shipped defaults**, read from `glowtuning.ts` and `skydim.ts` in the viewer's `main`
  and matching what Max reported:

| Setting | Shipped default |
|---|---|
| Glow gain | 0.20 |
| Glow saturation | 0.60 |
| Glow gamma | 1.35 |
| Glow blur | 0 (no blur; the background blur work is skipped at 0) |
| Earth in view, sunlit (glow and stars) | 1.00 |
| Earth in view, night (glow and stars) | 0.19 |
| Star brightness | 1.10 |
| Star point size | 1.00 |
| Wide-field limiting-magnitude offset | +3.5 mag (at fields wider than 15 degrees, fading to 0 by 5) |

"Original look" in the panel is the look before the softening (glow 1.0 / 0.7 / 1.5, no blur,
dimming off, stars neutral).

## The 90 degree field-of-view cap (#39)

A camera's field of view is its vertical angle. On a wide map the horizontal angle is far larger
(100 degrees vertical on a 16:9 map is about 130 across; the viewer README's example), and the edges
stretch like a fish-eye lens. Max reported about 119 degrees across at the largest zoom-out on the
wide map, which is the case that prompted it; that figure is Max's observation, not something
checkable from the repos. `fovcap.ts` caps the larger of the horizontal and vertical angles at 90
degrees by default (on a portrait phone the vertical one). The cap bounds zoom-out, the
constellation fit and the starting view; zooming in is unchanged. Forward is tipped down a little
so the limb stays in the narrower view. A debug slider (45 to 140 degrees; above about 100 the edges
visibly distort) sets it live.
