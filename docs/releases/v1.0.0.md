# v1.0.0 - Satellite Point-of-View Tool

The first release of the tool: a daily screening of public tracking data for close approaches
between satellites, and a browser viewer that lets you ride along with any tracked satellite and see
what it sees. This repo is the data side; the viewer is
[`portfolio-site`](https://github.com/maxnorris01-bot/portfolio-site).

## What it is

- **A screening pipeline** (this repo, Python). CelesTrak orbital elements for about 19,000 objects,
  propagated with SGP4, screened for pairs within 5 km over 24 hours with a `scipy` `cKDTree`, each
  pass refined to its time and miss distance, and rated by a documented threshold table. It makes
  zero LLM calls and costs $0 per run.
- **A daily run.** A GitHub Actions workflow starts a Fly.io Machine, which publishes the report,
  the object list and a 7-day history to a public Tigris bucket.
- **A star-data build** (`scripts/star_data/`). Gaia DR3 stars brighter than G=13 (7,251,917, minus
  those already in Hipparcos) in 768 HEALPix tiles plus a glow atlas, under the immutable bucket
  prefix `stars/v1/`.
- **A viewer** (`portfolio-site`, three.js). Globe, Sky and Satellite views, described below.

## What is in it

- Globe of every tracked object, with a time slider, near-miss replay, filters and station views.
- Sky view of what is above any place on Earth.
- The view from a satellite: heading-up or north-up, Earth imagery (EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless 2016
  and NASA GIBS), the Sun, Moon and planets at their true direction, descend zoom, and pause and
  replay.
- Real stars: Hipparcos plus streamed Gaia G<13, selectable with info cards, constellation figures,
  names and boundaries, and a Milky Way glow built from the faint Gaia sources.
- Sky dimming when looking at the Earth over the night side, and a `?debug=1` Sky tuning panel.
- A page layout that fits any window, with the controls in an overlay panel, and a 90 degree field of
  view cap.

## Known limitations

- The risk tiers are a heuristic, not a probability of collision. Public data has no covariance.
- TLE/SGP4 position error (about 1 km for fresh LEO elements) is as large as the thresholds, and
  maneuvers are invisible, so many `high` results (mostly Starlink at full-catalog scope) are not
  real near-misses.
- The Earth imagery is the 2016 Sentinel-2 composite.
- Gaia is a G<13 subset; fainter stars appear only as glow.
- Frame rate was checked in software GL only; real-GPU and phone performance with about 1M stars are
  unmeasured.
- Vercel preview deployments cannot load the bucket data (CORS), so only production shows it.
- The v1.1 list is in [`docs/todo.md`](../todo.md#v11-polish-and-backlog).
